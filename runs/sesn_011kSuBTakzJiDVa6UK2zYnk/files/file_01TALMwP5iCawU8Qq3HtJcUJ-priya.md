# Discovery call transcript: Priya (Lumen Skin)

Q: What are you trying to get an agent to do, and for whom?
A: Honestly, I want it to basically be my ops manager at night. It's for me and my team at Lumen Skin, we're a nine-person DTC skincare brand. The idea is it checks our Shopify inventory against how long each supplier takes to deliver, then drafts the reorder emails. It would be amazing if it could just email suppliers for me.

Q: How does reordering get done at Lumen Skin today, without an agent?
A: Mostly me, late at night. I pull up Shopify, eyeball what's running low, work out in my head which suppliers are slow, and write the emails myself. It works, it's just a lot of my evenings.

Q: Tell me about the last time you tried to build or run an agent for this. Where did it get stuck?
A: Getting it hooked up to Shopify was the first wall. I literally pasted my Shopify key into the prompt because I couldn't figure out where else it goes. I know that's bad. It kind of worked, but I didn't love it.

Q: What did you try before pasting the key into the prompt?
A: Honestly, not much. I poked around looking for somewhere obvious to put it, didn't find anything that made sense to me, and I was tired. So I just dropped it in the prompt and moved on.

Q: You called Shopify the first wall. What happened after you got past it?
A: Once it could see the inventory, I set it up to run every night on its own. That was the whole point, not having to babysit it.

Q: Walk me through the last night it ran on its own. What happened?
A: That's the annoying part. It ran every night but kept checking stock for the 14th. I'd typed the date into the task and it just replayed it forever. So every morning I'd get a "report" that was basically the same stale check.

Q: What did those stale reports end up costing you?
A: Mostly my time and my trust in it. Once I noticed, I stopped believing anything it told me and went back to checking Shopify myself at night. So I was basically doing the job twice for a while.

Q: If it worked perfectly next week, what would you see in the morning?
A: Oh, that would be amazing. I'd wake up to a short list of what's running low today, which suppliers need to hear from us given their lead times, and the reorder emails already drafted. I'd just skim, hit send, and get on with my day. Basically my ops manager did the night shift.
