# Discovery call transcript: Ben

Q: What are you trying to get an agent to do, and for whom?
A: So I run a research newsletter for climate investors, just me, totally self-funded. I want a daily agent that goes through new papers and funding news and writes me a first draft of the newsletter. Honestly it's basically my research intern that never sleeps. And I want it to have taste, you know? Pick the stuff that actually matters to people putting money into climate.

Q: How does that get done today, without the agent?
A: Right now it's mostly me with way too much coffee at 6am, going through arXiv, a few journals, and funding announcements, and pasting the good bits into a doc. Then I write the thing myself, which eats most of my morning. I can't really afford to hire an actual human intern, so that's why I'm keen to get an agent doing the grunt work.

Q: Tell me about the last time you tried to build or run an agent for this. Where did it get stuck?
A: Oh man, so I hacked together a version a couple months back and let it run every morning. One run went down a rabbit hole and cost me more than the whole previous week. I had no cap on it. When you're paying for everything out of your own pocket that really stings, so I shut it off pretty quick after that.

Q: Walk me through that run. What was it doing when it went down the rabbit hole?
A: From what I could piece together after, it found one paper on grid-scale storage and just kept following the references, paper after paper, reading and summarizing each one. There was nothing telling it "okay, that's enough, go write the draft," so it just kept going. I only noticed when I checked my bill, and I actually said "wait, what?" out loud in my kitchen.

Q: How did you piece together what it had been doing?
A: Honestly, mostly by scrolling through the output it dumped into my doc that morning, which was this enormous pile of paper summaries. I matched that against the usage on my bill and it was pretty obvious what had happened. Not exactly scientific, but it got me there.

Q: What has having it switched off cost you since then?
A: Mostly my mornings, I'm back to doing the whole scan and write-up by hand, which is a few hours a day I'd rather spend growing the thing. It's also cheaper in the literal sense, ha, but it means I'm not sending as much or as fast as I'd like. When it's just you, time is basically the only thing you've got.

Q: If it worked perfectly next week, what would you see?
A: Oh, that'd be the dream! I'd wake up, grab a coffee, and there'd be a tight draft waiting with the five or six things climate investors actually need to know that day, picked with real taste. I'd spend twenty minutes polishing it, hit send, and use the rest of the morning to grow the business. My research intern that never sleeps, finally doing its job!

Q: Anything I should have asked but didn't?
A: Hmm, good question! Maybe what the drafts themselves were like, since we mostly talked about that one expensive run and not so much the day-to-day output. And maybe what it'd be worth to me in actual dollars, since I think about money constantly, ha.
